# Experiment 0033 — Wire repair control under a budget

- **Status:** complete (all gates pass; see [`summary.md`](summary.md))
- **Date:** 2026-11-21
- **Type:** P1 phase 1B control experiment
- **Mechanic:** wired power topology and deterministic wire faults (RFC 0007,
  ADR 0017); repair action (this experiment)
- **Task:** `factory.power.wire-repair.v1` (debug/P1, not in `factory-v0.1`)
- **Benchmark:** none; frozen `factory-v0.1` unchanged

## Question

Can a budget-aware policy prioritize wire repairs by restoration impact and
avoid spending a repair on a zero-impact loop?

The repair decision is graph-dependent: a bridge fault disconnects a downstream
component; a loop fault reroutes power and restores nothing when repaired. With
three scheduled faults and a two-repair budget, the agent must skip the loop and
keep budget for both bridges.

## Environment

- Gear line, wired power, one generator, capacity 12 (connectivity is isolated
  from allocation stress).
- Three scheduled faults:
  - tick 50: a zero-impact loop edge;
  - tick 150: a small-outage bridge;
  - tick 250: a large-outage bridge.
- Fault roles are chosen per layout from the outage audit and are deterministic.
- Repair budget: 2, one repair per decision interval.
- Repair cost: 1; restored-consumer reward: 10 per consumer; wasted-repair
  penalty: 20.
- **Reward:** `restored_consumers * 10 - 1 - 20 * 1[outage == 0]`.
- **Outcome metric:** final production score; success ≥ 150.
- Decision interval 10; episode 500 ticks.

## Observation and actions

18 features:

- global: tick, score, budget remaining, failed count, max outage, total outage;
- three ranked slots: outage, is-bridge, age, exists.

Slots are sorted by restoration impact, so slot 1 is the highest-impact failed
wire (which may be a zero-impact loop).

Actions: `noop`, `repair slot 1`, `repair slot 2`, `repair slot 3`. Slots are
masked when empty or when the budget is exhausted.

## Baselines

| Baseline | Rule |
|---|---|
| noop | never repair |
| repair_now | repair the first failed slot whenever possible |
| repair_largest | repair slot 1 only when its outage is positive |
| random | uniform over legal actions |
| tabular Q | 300 training layouts; ε-greedy; state = budget, failed count, outage buckets |

Q training seeds start at 2000 and are disjoint from evaluation seed ranges.

## Data and seeds

- Evaluation seeds: 100–104; 5 episodes each = 25 episodes.
- Q training: 300 layouts starting at 2000 + 1000 × Q seed.
- No dataset artifact; layouts are generated by the environment.

## Gates

| Gate | Threshold |
|---|---|
| H1 repair gain | repair_largest score ≥ noop + 100 |
| H2 impact-aware gain | repair_largest score ≥ repair_now + 100 |
| H3 Q quality | Q score ≥ 0.70 × repair_largest |
| H4 Q success | Q success rate ≥ 0.60 |
| H5 budget discipline | Q wasted repairs ≤ 0.10 per episode; report budget |

## Predictions

- Noop scores about 80; repair_now about 120 with one wasted repair.
- repair_largest scores about 260 and succeeds on every seed.
- Q learns to skip zero-impact loops, scores about 200–220, and succeeds on
  0.7–0.8 of episodes.
- Random matches repair_now because it usually repairs the loop as well.

## Kill criteria

- repair_largest does not beat noop by 50.
- repair_now does not waste a repair on the loop.
- Q is below 60% of repair_largest or succeeds below 0.5.
- The result is dominated by one seed.
- The task requires post-hoc changes to the fault schedule, reward, or gates.

## Analysis plan

- Report mean score with bootstrap intervals, mean reward, repairs, wasted
  repairs, and success rate.
- Report per-seed scores for every policy.
- Report the schedule (loop/small/large) and budget.
- Do not tune the fault schedule, reward, or gates after the formal run.
- Keep wired mode opt-in and `factory-v0.1` frozen.
