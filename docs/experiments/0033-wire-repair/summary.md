# Experiment 0033 — Wire repair control under a budget

- **Status:** complete (all gates pass)
- **Date:** 2026-11-21
- **Type:** P1 phase 1B control experiment
- **Mechanic:** wired power topology and deterministic wire faults (RFC 0007,
  ADR 0017); instant repair action
- **Task:** `factory.power.wire-repair.v1` (debug/P1, not in `factory-v0.1`)
- **Code:** `src/fabline/env/wire_repair.py`,
  `src/fabline/harness/wire_repair.py`
- **Pre-registration:** [`preregistration.md`](preregistration.md)
- **Benchmark:** none; frozen `factory-v0.1` unchanged

## Result in one paragraph

**Impact-aware repair beats both noop and repair-now, and tabular Q learns to
skip the zero-impact loop.** With three scheduled faults (loop, small bridge,
large bridge) and a two-repair budget, outage-ranked repair scores **260** with
success 1.00, noop scores 79.2, and repair-now scores 121.6 while wasting one
repair on the loop. Tabular Q scores **212.0** (81.5% of outage-ranked), wastes
**0** repairs, and succeeds on **0.68** of episodes. All five gates pass. Q is
seed-sensitive: per-seed scores are 260, 178, 242, 232, and 148, so the result is
a solid positive with one weak seed rather than a uniform solve.

## Environment

- Gear line, wired power, one generator, capacity 12.
- Three faults:
  - tick 50: zero-impact loop edge;
  - tick 150: small-outage bridge;
  - tick 250: large-outage bridge.
- Repair budget 2; one repair per decision interval; instant repair.
- Reward: `restored_consumers * 10 - 1 - 20 * 1[outage == 0]`.
- Outcome: final production score; success ≥ 150.
- Observation: 18 features; slots ranked by restoration impact.
- Actions: noop or repair slot 1–3, masked by budget and slot existence.

## Data and seeds

- Evaluation: seeds 100–104, 5 episodes each = 25 episodes.
- Q training: 300 layouts from seed 2000 + 1000 × Q seed; disjoint from
  evaluation.
- No dataset artifact; layouts are generated by the environment.

## Interpretation

- **The loop trap is real.** Repair-now and random always spend one repair on
  the zero-impact loop and never reach the success threshold.
- **Restoration impact is enough to solve the task.** Outage-ranked repair is
  optimal on every seed.
- **Q learns the budget discipline.** It wastes no repairs and beats both
  non-learned baselines, though it misses the optimal policy on the weak seed.
- **Reward shaping was necessary.** A production-delta reward made the budget
  trade too delayed for tabular Q; restored-consumer reward plus a wasted-repair
  penalty gives immediate credit. Production score remains the outcome metric.
- **The action abstraction is ranked slots.** This is a prioritization task, not
  end-to-end graph control. The graph reasoning is in the outage computation.

## Caveats

- One deterministic fault schedule (loop, small, large) and one budget.
- Slots are ranked by restoration impact; wire identity is hidden.
- Instant repair; no crew travel or repair duration.
- One generator and one inlet per consumer.
- Q is seed-sensitive (one weak seed at 148 / 0.20 success).
- Success 0.68 is above the gate but not a uniform solve.
- Production score is the outcome, not the reward.
- Not part of frozen `factory-v0.1`.

## Next

1. **Repair time or crew travel:** make the timing trade explicit.
2. **End-to-end graph repair policy:** choose a wire node instead of a ranked
   slot.
3. **Multi-generator repair:** alternate paths and redundancy.
4. **Wire capacity/overload trips:** a second failure mechanism.
5. Keep `factory-v0.1` frozen.
