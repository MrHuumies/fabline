# Experiment 0028 — Reliability-contract selection under stochastic power

- **Status:** complete (H3 fails; partial negative)
- **Date:** 2026-11-21
- **Type:** P0 risk-sensitive control experiment
- **Task:** `factory.power.contract.v1`
- **Code:** `experiments/risk_control/contract.py`
- **Pre-registration:** [`preregistration.md`](preregistration.md)
- **Benchmark:** none; `factory-v0.1` unchanged

## Result in one paragraph

**The mean/CVaR tradeoff exists, but the pre-registered tail gain does not
replicate at the 20% tail.** Train selects target 130 by mean (142.15) and
target 120 by CVaR₂₀ (126.12), and both orderings hold on test, so H1, H2, H4,
and H5 pass. On the held-out test block, however, the CVaR-optimal target gains
only **+2.5** over the mean-optimal target (106.5 vs 104.0), below the +15 gate;
H3 fails. The effect is concentrated in the extreme tail: an exploratory
(non-gated) analysis gives +15.0 at CVaR₁₀ and +20.0 at CVaR₀₅. The test block is
also structurally clustered — seeds 80–94 are benign, while seeds 95–99 each
contain one zero-score episode — so the 20% tail estimate mixes a benign bulk
with a clustered failure mode. This is a stability boundary, not evidence that
risk-sensitive selection is useless.

## Contract

- Each episode runs the stochastic power-allocation task with the scripted
  alternator:
  - gear line; one generator; capacity 5; radius 8;
  - 500 ticks; decision interval 10; success threshold 100;
  - load jitter 30, capacity jitter 20, period 20.
- Target tiers: 120, 125, 130, 135, 140, 145, 150, 155.
- Payoff for target `T`:
  - production score `s`;
  - bonus `T - 120` when `s >= T`;
  - miss penalty 40 when `s < T`.
- The target does not affect the world; it changes only the payoff.
- Selection is offline over logged scores, not online bandit learning.

## Data

- Train: seeds 40–79, 10 episodes each = 400 scores.
- Test: seeds 80–99, 10 episodes each = 200 scores.
- Design pilot: seeds 0–39 (not formal evidence).
- No dataset artifact; logs are regenerated by the runner.

Selected policies on test:

| policy | test mean | test CVaR₂₀ | test worst |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| mean-optimal (130) | **138.90** | 104.00 | −40.00 |
| CVaR-optimal (120) | 131.40 | **106.50** | −40.00 |

The risk-safe target gives up 7.50 mean points for 2.50 CVaR points on this
test block.

## Gates

| Gate | Threshold | Result |
|---|---|---|
| H1 tradeoff | train mean target > train CVaR target | **pass**, 130 > 120 |
| H2 mean cost | mean-optimal test mean ≥ CVaR-optimal − 5 | **pass**, 138.90 vs 126.40 |
| H3 tail gain | CVaR-optimal test CVaR ≥ mean-optimal + 15 | **fail**, +2.50 |
| H4 target stability | train and test optima match | **pass** |
| H5 no free lunch | neither target dominates | **pass** |

## Exploratory tail diagnostic (not gated)

The same test payoffs at smaller tail fractions:

| alpha | target 120 CVaR | target 130 CVaR | gain |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0.05 | 40.00 | 20.00 | +20.00 |
| 0.10 | 83.00 | 68.00 | +15.00 |
| 0.20 | 106.50 | 104.00 | +2.50 |
| 0.30 | 114.33 | 116.00 | −1.67 |

The risk premium is in the extreme tail. Changing alpha after the formal run
would be post-hoc; this table diagnoses where the effect lives, it does not
rescue H3.

## Test-block structure

| seeds | behavior |
|---|---|
| 80–94 | benign; scores 120–140; no zeros |
| 95–99 | each has one zero-score episode; block mean 122–123 |

The failures are clustered by seed, not spread through the block. A 200-episode
tail estimate therefore depends on which seeds are in the test set. The design
pilot (seeds 20–39) had a much heavier tail and showed a +26 CVaR gain at
alpha 0.2; the formal block did not.

## Interpretation

- **The decision tradeoff is real.** Mean and CVaR select different targets, and
  the ordering is stable across train and test.
- **The tail effect is smaller and more concentrated than predicted.** At the
  pre-registered 20% tail, the risk-safe target buys only 2.5 points.
- **Seed clustering is the main threat.** Five contiguous seeds produce all the
  zeros; the rest are benign. This is not i.i.d. tail sampling.
- **A bigger mean reward for the riskier contract.** Target 130 earns 7.5 more
  mean points on test, but its worst outcomes are worse. Whether that is a good
  trade is exactly the risk preference the task is meant to expose.
- **This is a partial negative for the benchmark, not for risk sensitivity.**
  The task is not yet stable enough to be a headline risk benchmark.

## Caveats

- One jitter regime, one operating policy (scripted alternator), one miss
  penalty, one alpha.
- Offline selection; no online bandit learning.
- 200 test episodes; the tail is dominated by 5 zero-score episodes.
- The test block is clustered by seed; treat CIs and tail estimates cautiously.
- The target does not affect the world; this is a commitment decision, not
  sequential control.
- The miss penalty and tiers were chosen in a design pilot on seeds 0–39, then
  frozen before the formal run.

## Next

1. **A regime with more frequent misses** — higher load jitter, lower capacity,
   or a tighter contract — so the tail is sampled, not clustered.
2. **Pre-register the tail fraction** from the regime, not from a pilot effect
   size; consider CVaR₁₀ with a blocked evaluation design.
3. **Online bandit or contextual selection** only after the offline task is
   stable.
4. Keep `factory-v0.1` frozen.
