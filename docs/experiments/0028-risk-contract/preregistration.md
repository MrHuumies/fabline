# Experiment 0028 — Reliability-contract selection under stochastic power

- **Status:** complete (H3 fails; see [`summary.md`](summary.md))
- **Date:** 2026-11-21
- **Type:** P0 risk-sensitive control experiment
- **Task:** `factory.power.contract.v1` (debug/P0, not in `factory-v0.1`)
- **Mechanic:** stochastic power allocation (30/20/20) plus a fixed operating
  policy
- **Benchmark:** none; `factory-v0.1` unchanged

## Question

When a factory must commit to a delivery target before an episode runs, does a
risk-sensitive selection rule choose a different target than a mean-optimal
rule, and what does tail safety cost in mean payoff?

This is a one-shot **commitment** decision layered on the existing power
allocation task. The target does not affect the world; it changes only the
payoff. The experiment compares offline selection rules over logged episode
scores, not online bandit learning.

## Contract

- Each episode runs `PowerAllocationEnv` with the scripted alternator policy:
  - gear line; one generator; capacity 5; radius 8;
  - 500 ticks; decision interval 10; success threshold 100;
  - load jitter 30, capacity jitter 20, period 20.
- Target tiers: `120, 125, 130, 135, 140, 145, 150, 155`.
- Payoff for target `T`:
  - production score `s`;
  - bonus `T - 120` when `s >= T`;
  - miss penalty `40` when `s < T`.
  - `payoff = s + (T - 120) * 1[s >= T] - 40 * 1[s < T]`.
- The fixed miss penalty is a contract term. It was chosen in the design pilot
  (seeds 0–39) as the smallest penalty that produced a stable train/test
  separation; it is not tuned against the formal seeds.

## Data

- Training log: seeds 40–79, 10 episodes each = 400 scores.
- Test log: seeds 80–99, 10 episodes each = 200 scores.
- Design pilot: seeds 0–39, not reported as formal evidence.
- Scores come from the deterministic seeded stochastic-power schedules.
- No dataset artifact; the logs are regenerated by the runner.

## Selection rules

| Rule | Choice |
|---|---|
| mean-optimal | target with the highest mean payoff on train |
| CVaR-optimal | target with the highest CVaR₂₀ payoff on train |

CVaR₂₀ is the mean of the worst 20% of payoffs. Ties go to the lower target.

## Gates

| Gate | Threshold |
|---|---|
| H1 tradeoff | train mean-optimal target > train CVaR-optimal target |
| H2 mean cost | test mean of mean-optimal ≥ test mean of CVaR-optimal − 5 |
| H3 tail gain | test CVaR of CVaR-optimal ≥ test CVaR of mean-optimal + 15 |
| H4 target stability | the mean- and CVaR-optimal targets are the same on train and test |
| H5 no free lunch | neither target dominates the other on both test mean and test CVaR |

## Predictions

- The mean-optimal target will be **130**; the CVaR-optimal target will be
  **120**.
- The risk-safe target will cost about 2–4 mean points on test.
- The risk-safe target will gain about 25 CVaR points on test.
- The target ordering will be stable across train and test; the design pilot
  showed this at 400/200 episodes.
- Fixed targets above 135 will have poor CVaR because misses pay the penalty.

## Analysis plan

- Report every tier's train and test mean, CVaR, hit rate, and worst payoff.
- Report both selection rules and all five gates.
- Report the score distribution (mean, std, quantiles, zero rate).
- Do not change the tiers, penalty, alpha, or seeds after the formal run.
- If the tradeoff fails to replicate, report the negative in the first
  paragraph; do not search for a payoff that works.

## Kill criteria

- Mean- and CVaR-optimal targets coincide on train.
- The risk-safe target costs more than 10 mean points.
- The tail gain is below 10 CVaR points.
- The target ordering is unstable between train and test.
- The result requires post-hoc changes to the payoff or tiers.
